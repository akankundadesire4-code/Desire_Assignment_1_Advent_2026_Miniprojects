Mini-Project 3: Lake Victoria Fish Stock & Export Risk Model
====================================================
Plain-language notes for questions the assignment asks us to explain.

1. Why is unbounded Fibonacci growth biologically unrealistic?
   - Fibonacci numbers grow without any limit - each number keeps getting
     bigger forever, following a fixed mathematical rule.
   - Real fish stocks live in a lake with limited food, space and oxygen.
     A real population cannot grow forever; it must slow down and level
     off near some maximum the environment can support (its "carrying
     capacity"). Fibonacci numbers have no concept of this limit, so using
     them to model "stock" ignores basic biology and would predict an
     impossible, ever-growing fish population.
   - The logistic growth model we use instead includes a carrying capacity
     K, so growth naturally slows down as the stock approaches K.

2. Why is a raw variance threshold like 50,000 meaningless here?
   - Variance is measured in squared units. If revenue is in UGX, then the
     variance of revenue is in UGX-squared, not UGX. A number like 50,000
     UGX-squared has no natural real-world interpretation, and it cannot be
     compared across datasets with different units or scales.
   - The coefficient of variation (CV = standard deviation / mean) has no
     units at all - it is just a ratio - so it can be sensibly compared
     across different revenue scales and used to set a fair risk threshold
     ("high risk if CV > 0.35", for example), unlike a raw variance cutoff.

3. How does our harvest rate compare to the Maximum Sustainable Yield (MSY)?
   - The theoretical MSY for the logistic model is MSY = r * K / 4.
   - The notebook compares total revenue and final stock for harvest rates
     h = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20 and 0.30 against this MSY figure. Harvest rates
     that take the stock well below half of K for long periods, or push
     final stock towards zero, are not sustainable even if they produce
     high revenue in the short term - see the notebook's scenario table for
     the exact comparison with this data.

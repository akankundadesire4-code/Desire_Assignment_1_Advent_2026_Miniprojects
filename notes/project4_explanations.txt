Mini-Project 4: Rainfall Pattern & Crop Suitability Analyser
====================================================
Plain-language notes for questions the assignment asks us to explain.

1. Why was math.cos() the wrong tool for "cosine similarity" last year?
   - math.cos(x) computes the trigonometric cosine of a single angle x (in
     radians). It takes one number and returns one number.
   - Cosine similarity compares two vectors (lists of numbers, like 12
     months of rainfall) and tells you how similar their *shape* is,
     ignoring their overall scale. The correct formula is:
     cosine_similarity(a, b) = (a . b) / (|a| * |b|)
     where "a . b" is the dot product and |a|, |b| are vector lengths
     (norms). This is implemented in src/project4_rainfall.py and checked
     against scipy.spatial.distance.cosine in the tests and notebook.

2. Why can cosine similarity call two regions "similar" even if one is much
   wetter?
   - Cosine similarity only compares the *pattern* (relative shape) of the
     12 monthly values, not their size. Two regions where one always gets
     roughly twice as much rain as the other, but follows the same wet/dry
     month pattern, will have a cosine similarity close to 1 (very
     similar), even though their totals are very different.
   - This is why the notebook also reports Euclidean distance (which does
     care about scale) and Pearson correlation, so we do not rely on
     cosine similarity alone.

3. How do we detect rainy seasons and classify unimodal/bimodal regions?
   - scipy.signal.find_peaks() scans the 12 monthly values and finds local
     maxima ("peaks") that stand out from their neighbours by at least a
     chosen "prominence" and are a minimum "distance" apart, so short
     one-month wiggles are not counted as separate seasons.
   - A region with one detected peak is classified "unimodal" (one rainy
     season); a region with two is classified "bimodal" (two rainy
     seasons). We compare this to Uganda's known climate zones: areas near
     the Lake Victoria basin are typically bimodal (two rainy seasons),
     while parts of northern Uganda are typically closer to unimodal.
